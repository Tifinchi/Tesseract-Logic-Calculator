Tests for chance, applied to both

Agreement proves little if an alphabet of the same density lands as close by accident. Each standard prediction was one number computed before its measurement, so its chance is the width of its window, ±max(|pull|, 1)σ, against a prior range of values. The calculator's chances come from the look-elsewhere test of its alphabet. Bits are −log₂ p, and each group is combined by Fisher's method.

The verdict through the four cores

Tifinchi's Quad-Core rule puts every claim through four cores: outcome, constraint, alternative and consequence. No core overrides another and there is no majority: one rejection rejects the claim, four passes stabilise it, and anything else leaves it unstable. The state column is computed from the cores by that rule.

What decides it: the nine frozen predictions

Each prediction was frozen with its definition before its data. Enter a decider's result m and its error σ_m. The score is the Bayes factor B = N(m; c, √(σ_c² + σ_m²)) ÷ p₀(m), where p₀ is today's value taken as a split normal and spread by σ_m. The calculator's own seven multiply: above 100 is decisive for its numbers, below 1/100 decisive against, and anything between is open. Refutation is a range on two yardsticks: the pull |m − c| ÷ √(σ_c² + σ_m²), with σ_c the calculation's precision as frozen, and the same pull with σ_c′, the calculation at the input precision its decider brings. Above 5 on both refutes the prediction's rule; above 5 on the second alone puts the result between the lines. Only the top carries a precision of its own: ±0.38 GeV frozen, ±0.15 GeV with α_s to ±0.0002. The two prior-art predictions are scored apart.

rate = J · ∇H · one law for every layer

The law of motion

The quarter turn J, applied to the gradient of the sum table, moves every state: ẋ = ∂H/∂p and ṗ = −∂H/∂x. Near a mass the sum table sits on two legs, the time factor A and the space factor B: H = √A·√(m²c⁴ + p²c²/B). A slow body feels mostly the time leg; light takes both. For amplitudes the same turn is ψ̇ = −(i/ħ)Hψ. This is Hamilton's, Einstein's and Schrödinger's law exactly, so it adds no number of its own. What it changes is that the calculator computes motion instead of labelling it: every row below is integrated here, exactly, from turning point to turning point.

4.4 × 10²⁰ permutations · nine families · one rule

Every permutation

Each relation the calculator uses is built from a few choices: a balance angle, a phase, a frame and its tilt, a load, a share, a crossing, a fraction. Every choice has alternatives inside the cube's own sets, and a permutation is one choice for each. Every permutation was scored by the behavior rule: it is carried to every measurement it reaches, and it stays only if no deciding measurement misses by more than 2σ; a miss that every permutation in a family shares is inherited and eliminates nothing. The survivors were then tested on measurements they were not chosen with, and against the chance that a family of their size matches random values as well. Level √k = k phases on

The cube, all together

On the side-2 cube a point's distance from the centre is √k, where k is how many of its three coordinates are switched on. So each level counts active phases: none at the centre, one on the face points, two on the edges, all three at the corners, and the held leg at √4. Every piece sits on one level or one move: your rules, the reactive board, Bass's vacuum and the measurements.

The roots: level, inverse, grid, move

Each level √k has an inverse 1/√k, and the inverse squared is the grid, 1/k: the share one leg carries of the diagonal (cos² of the leg angle). The move that belongs to each level is the k-th root of 1, and it is the cube's own axis through that level's points.

One generation on the tesseract: the ⅓ grid on the ¼ grid

Five slots: three colour (−⅓ each, the ⅓ grid) and two weak (+½ each, the ½ grid), with an even number filled, so four are free: the tesseract's 16 corners. Y is the hypercharge, T₃ the weak spin, Q = Y + T₃ the charge.

Read as data: each corner of the cube is a three-bit word (the sign of each axis; Harari–Shupe's T and V), eight words with counts (1, 3, 3, 1) by set bits and charges 1, ⅔, ⅓, 0. In both directions (inside-out: left- and right-handed, equally particle and antiparticle) that is 16. The flip draws it: the tesseract seen in 3D is the cube in both directions of the flip, 8 corners at √3 outside the unit sphere and 8 at 1/√3 inside, on the cube of half-side ⅓; in general level k flips onto the cube of half-side 1/k, which is where the grids come from, and the 4-cube's corner at 2 flips to ½ on a 4-cube of half-side ¼ (the ⅓ grid on the ¼ grid). The same 8 and 16 are the Clifford algebras of 3-space and 4-space, graded (1, 3, 3, 1) and (1, 4, 6, 4, 1): scalar, directions, planes of turning, inside-out; the six planes of 4-space are the three turns and three boosts. Exact; same as standard.

Read as a machine it is a reversible computer: the 48 operations are every bit permutation times every bit complement of a 3-bit word, generated by the three moves (NOT on all bits, a cyclic shift, a swap with one NOT); the 16 is the even-parity code of length 5, with the anomaly sums as its parity checks; three inside-outs in a ring oscillate 120° apart (the ring oscillator, the three-phase start) and two in a ring hold (the latch, the held level); h is the action per clock cycle and one i-turn takes at least h/4E (Margolus–Levitin). Structure, not numbers; the lineage is Zuse, Feynman, Fredkin, Wheeler, Lloyd.

Face points r = 1 · the fixed sphere of the flip

Black hole as inversion

Black-hole entrance, no exit. Einstein's solution in isotropic form is unchanged by ρ → ρ₀²/ρ with ρ₀ = GM/2c². The fixed sphere is the horizon, where the clock rate is 0: the non-action sphere. Each point outside has a partner inside with the same area and the opposite clock sign, but the partner is not an exit: inside, the inward direction becomes time, so what looks to us like inaction is motion along the perpendicular, and what falls in is held; the only return is on the hyp, as light. The torus sits on the black-hole side (Kerr's ring singularity is a ring), and the passage through it is sealed by the inner horizon's instability (mass inflation, Poisson and Israel 1990). From outside, everything that fell in is frozen at the horizon by time dilation. The only return is Hawking's glow. Consistent with no white hole ever seen.

1 is the horizon itself. 4 sits outside; its partner sits at 1/4 on the other side.
