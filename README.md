Abstract
The Tesseract Logic Calculator is the author's system for computing with a cube of side 2. Its 27 points sit
at distances √k from the centre, its eight corners carry delimiters that in the electrical frame name
charge, current, flux and voltage, and every derivative, inverse or change of scale is a quarter or half turn
of the tesseract that holds the cube. This paper uses it to compute the structure of reality layer by layer,
from the vacuum to the cosmos, and scores every result against measurement on one yardstick, beside
the standard theories' result for the same quantity. The calculator rebuilds four established structures
exactly: the Standard Model's generation of matter as a 16-state register, the energy–momentum
relation and interval of special relativity, the classical tests of gravity and the shells of the atom. Where
the standard theories take inputs it gives numbers, among them the τ mass from the electron and muon
(1,776.969 MeV, −0.4σ), the top and Higgs masses (172.57 ± 0.38 and 125.20 ± 0.92 GeV), neutrino
mixing from a 15° tilt (sin²θ₁₂ = 0.318, −1.5σ), the matter share π²/32 (−1.2σ) and the vacuum's energy
scale (2.02 ± 0.20 meV, +1.1σ). All 22 committed numbers sit within 1.6σ of their primary
measurements. Five tests fixed before they were run, applied to both theories, find the calculator exact
as notation and its numbers indistinguishable from chance: alphabets built from random ingredients
match 19 of 29 outside constants as often as its alphabet does (P = 0.79), and its own numbers carry 0.6
to 4.0 bits against chance, while the standard theories' eleven predictions of independent measurements
carry 102 bits. The standard theories also carry six failures, among them the vacuum's energy and the
Hubble constant. Under the author's Quad-Core rule, neither the claim that the calculator is how reality
works nor the claim that the standard theories are complete stands today. Nine predictions are frozen
with a Bayes-factor rule, and JUNO's sin²θ₁₂ (about 2031) is the first to decide.
Keywords: tesseract; difference of squares; root families; Koide relation; tribimaximal mixing; vacuum
energy; look-elsewhere effect; pre-registration; Bayes factor; theory comparison.
